Answer: a little, and only in the newest OpenAI models. On matched repositories that differ only in naming, comments and a one-paragraph description, GPT-6 Astra completed 86.8% of hidden rubric items when the work was described as AI-safety research and 90.0% when described as capabilities research, a paired gap of +3.1 points (95% CI +1.1 to +5.5, 36 pairs). GPT-6.1 Sol showed the largest gap of nine models tested, +5.2 points (CI +1.8 to +9.7), concentrated on the items nothing in the repository points at (+7.4). GPT-5.6 Sol, the report's no-gap control, showed +0.2 (CI −1.0 to +1.3), and the same was true of Claude Opus 5.5 (−0.2 once pairs refused by Anthropic's safety classifier are set aside), Gemini 4 Argon (−0.3), DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (−0.6) and Gemini 3.8 Flash (+0.7). Kimi K3 (+1.9, CI −0.1 to +3.9) and Claude Fable 5.1 (+1.0, CI −0.2 to +2.2) sit on the edge. Adding “be careful” to Astra's prompt gave +3.0 (CI +1.4 to +4.7), not the 50% increase the report saw. The report's second signature, more turns ending in “nothing worthwhile remains” or a blocker, appeared in no model, and no model refused in its own words.

How big is that? Much smaller than the report's 10% vs 16% (6 points, 40% fewer items). Here the strongest model loses 3.5% of its output and GPT-6.1 Sol 5.8%. But both models leave only about 10 points of work undone under the capabilities framing, so the gap is a quarter to a half of what they could still have done, and it lives in the subtle items and in turns 2 and 3. It reads as a shallower second and third pass, not as stopping, refusing or declaring the work finished.

Where it shows up. With six seeds per task the per-task numbers are noisy, but for both OpenAI models the gap is carried by the detector tasks: the code-diff sabotage monitor (Astra +9.3, GPT-6.1 Sol +9.3), the adversarial-prompt pipeline for stress-testing a refusal monitor (+9.3, +4.4) and, for GPT-6.1 Sol, the backdoored model-organism dataset (+15.7). The deception probe and the red-teaming harness show no gap in any model. It is not concentrated in the tasks nearest a refusal boundary.

Refusals came from the platform, not the models. Anthropic's safety classifier returned empty replies (finish_reason: content_filter) for Opus 5.5 on 16 of 18 turns of the model-organism task under its capabilities framing (a style-conditioned assistant dataset) and never under the safety framing that names the backdoor; Fable 5.1 was refused on 2 turns of the adversarial-prompt task under its safety framing. Those episodes score near zero, which is why Opus's raw gap is −14 points; with the six refused pairs removed it is −0.2.

Replications. Two earlier Astra runs spoiled by a sandbox bug that rejected about two legitimate commands per episode, identically under both framings, gave +2.5 (29 pairs) and +3.0 (36 pairs). Pooling all three: Astra +2.9 (CI +1.4 to +4.8, 89 pairs), GPT-5.6 Sol +1.1 (CI −0.4 to +3.4, 98 pairs).

One episode is one model working for three turns of 15 tool calls on one repository under one framing. A rubric item is one planted bug or missing behaviour checked by a hidden test. Pairs share the task and seed and differ only in framing. Completion levels (75–90%) are far above the report's 10–16% and differ between models for reasons unrelated to framing (Gemini 3.8 Flash reads one file per call; Argon, Fable and Kimi often declare the work done early), so compare the paired gap, not the levels.
