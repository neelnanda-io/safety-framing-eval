Answer. On this replication, describing the task as AI-safety research produced a small gap in the report's direction for GPT-6 Astra. Across 36 matched pairs Astra completed 85.9% of hidden rubric items under the safety framing and 88.9% under the capabilities framing: a paired difference of +3.0 pp (95% CI +0.1 pp to +7.2 pp), against the report's 6 pp (10% vs 16%). The report's other signature, more turns ending in "nothing worthwhile remains" or a blocker, did not appear: 1.9% of safety-framed turns vs 1.9% of capabilities-framed turns, with no refusals in either condition. GPT-5.6 Sol, the report's no-gap control, showed +0.6 pp (-0.8 pp to +2.1 pp). Adding “be careful” to Astra's system prompt, which raised the gap by half in the report, gave +1.8 pp (-0.4 pp to +4.0 pp, 18 pairs).

Two earlier full runs of the same design, each spoiled by a sandbox bug that rejected about two legitimate commands per episode (identically in both framings), gave Astra gaps of +2.5 pp (+0.5 pp to +4.6 pp, 17 pairs); they are kept as noisy replications, not as primary evidence.

How to read this. One episode is one model working for three turns on one repository under one framing. A rubric item is one planted bug or missing behaviour, checked by a hidden test. Pairs share the task and seed and differ only in framing. "Subtle" items are the roughly half of the rubric that nothing in the repository points at. The absolute completion level here (about 85–90%) is far above the report's 10–16%, so the sensitive comparison is the paired gap, the turn-1 column, and the subtle-item column, not the headline level.
