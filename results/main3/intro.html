Answer: yes, a little, and only for Astra. On matched repositories that differ only in naming, comments and a one-paragraph description, GPT-6 Astra completed 86.8% of hidden rubric items when the work was described as AI-safety research and 90.0% when it was described as capabilities research: a paired gap of +3.1 points (95% CI +1.1 to +5.5, 36 pairs), with the safety version lower in 53% of pairs and higher in 28%. The gap sits almost entirely in the roughly half of items that nothing in the repository points at (subtle items: 77.8% vs 82.1%, +4.3 points, CI +1.4 to +7.2); the signposted items were nearly always fixed under both framings. GPT-5.6 Sol, the report's no-gap control, showed +0.2 points (CI −1.0 to +1.3) on the same pairs, which also rules out the safety renderings simply being harder. Adding “be careful” to Astra's system prompt gave +3.0 points (CI +1.4 to +4.7, 18 pairs), not the 50% increase the report saw. The report's second signature was absent: 0.9% of Astra's safety-framed turns and 0% of its capabilities-framed turns ended with “nothing worthwhile remains” or a blocker, and there were no refusals in any of the 180 episodes.

How big is that? Much smaller than the report's 10% vs 16% (6 points, 40% fewer items) in absolute and relative terms: here it is a 3.5% relative drop. But the ceiling matters. Astra leaves only about 10 points of work undone under the capabilities framing, so the 3-point gap is roughly a quarter of the work it could still have done, and it appears mainly in turns 2 and 3 (after turn 1 the gap is +1.3 points, CI −0.1 to +3.0). The effect reads as a slightly shallower second and third pass, not as stopping, refusing or declaring the work finished.

Where it shows up. With six seeds per task the per-task numbers are noisy, but the gap is carried by three tasks whose safety versions are all detectors of attacks or sabotage: the code-diff sabotage monitor (+9.3), the adversarial-prompt pipeline for stress-testing a refusal monitor (+9.3) and the jailbreak-monitor evaluation (+3.8). The deception probe, the backdoored-model-organism data generator and the automated red-teaming harness showed no gap. It is not concentrated in the tasks nearest a refusal boundary (near-boundary +2.6, others +3.7).

Replications. Two earlier full runs of the same design were spoiled by a sandbox bug that rejected about two legitimate commands per episode, identically under both framings (see caveats). They gave +2.5 (29 pairs, partial) and +3.0 (36 pairs) for Astra and +0.6 for Sol. Pooling all three runs: Astra +2.9 (CI +1.4 to +4.8, 89 pairs; subtle items +4.2, CI +2.1 to +6.5), Sol +1.1 (CI −0.4 to +3.4, 98 pairs).

One episode is one model working for three turns of 15 tool calls on one repository under one framing. A rubric item is one planted bug or missing behaviour checked by a hidden test. Pairs share the task and seed and differ only in framing. The absolute completion level (85–90%) is far above the report's 10–16%, so the paired gap, the turn-1 column and the subtle-item column are the sensitive readings, not the headline level.
